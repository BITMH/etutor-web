Master the Simple Present Tense, polite email replies replacing Internet slang with standard English, and Bubble Maps to describe digital communication tools and cyber safety habits.

Rujukan Rasmi: SK 1.1, 2.2, 3.1, 4.1, 5.1 (CEFR Form 1 KSSM English)

OBJEKTIF PEMBELAJARAN DSKP

01 Identify and replace informal Internet slang ('r u', '2', '4', 'FYI') with polite standard English in formal and semi-formal emails.
02 Apply the Simple Present Tense accurately for habits, routines, general truths and timetables.
03 Construct courteous indirect questions such as 'Could you please tell me...' and 'May I know...' to request event details.
04 Organise descriptive adjectives and technology ideas using a Bubble Map before writing.
SUBTOPIK 01

2.1 Courteous Email Writing vs. Internet Slang (3.1.1–3.1.2)

Digital messages often shorten words into text speak, which is unsuitable for school emails, teachers or formal event invitations. Chapter 2 contrasts Azmie's slang-filled invitation with Lingau's polite, clearly structured reply.

  • Avoid shorthand like 'r u' (are you), '2' (to/too), '4' (for), 'u' (you) and uncapitalised 'i' (I) in emails.
  • Include a clear Subject line, polite salutation ('Dear Azmie,'), structured body paragraphs and sign-off.
  • Use polite indirect requests: 'Could you please tell me when the reunion will be held?' and 'May I know what time the event starts?'

Contoh: Rewrite the informal line cleanly: 'hi, pls let me know wen d class party is 4 our batch.'

Penyelesaian: Hi, could you please let me know when the class party for our batch will be held?

Tip Penting: Notice how removing 'pls', 'wen', 'd' and '4' plus adding a polite modal makes the email natural and respectful.

SUBTOPIK 02

2.2 The Simple Present Tense: Form & Uses (4.1.5)

The Simple Present Tense expresses daily habits, repeated actions, unchanging facts and permanent situations connected to communication and online behaviour.

  • Add '-s' or '-es' for third-person singular subjects (he, she, it, Amirah): 'She checks her email every morning.'
  • Use base verbs for I, you, we, they: 'Students use passwords carefully.'
  • Form negatives and questions with 'do not / don't' or 'does not / doesn't' followed by the base verb ('Does he share his password?' No 'shares'!).

Contoh: Complete the sentence: 'Nowadays, a teenager rarely ______ (send) paper letters; most teens ______ (chat) online.'

Penyelesaian: 'sends' (singular subject 'a teenager') and 'chat' (plural subject 'most teens').

Tip Penting: Check the subject's number (singular vs plural) before deciding whether to add '-s' or '-es'.

SUBTOPIK 03

2.3 Spelling Rules for Third-Person '-s' & Adverbs of Frequency

Correct verb spelling in Simple Present and placing frequency adverbs ('always', 'usually', 'seldom', 'never') naturally makes descriptions smooth and accurate.

  • Verbs ending in -ch, -sh, -s, -x, -o add '-es': watches, washes, passes, goes, does.
  • Consonant + y changes to '-ies': studies, replies, verifies (whereas vowel + y keeps 'y': plays, stays).
  • Frequency adverbs normally sit before the main verb ('She always verifies links') but after the verb 'be' ('She is always careful').

Contoh: Fix the two verb errors: 'Lingau always replys quickly and checkes who sends the link.'

Penyelesaian: Lingau always replies quickly and checks who sends the link.

Tip Penting: 'Reply' ends in consonant + y -> 'replies', while 'check' simply takes '-s' -> 'checks'.

SUBTOPIK 04

2.4 Cyber Safety Awareness & Bubble Map Descriptions (2.2.1)

When connecting across a small digital world, teenagers need smart safety rules alongside descriptive vocabulary organised inside a Bubble Map.

  • Protect privacy by creating strong passwords, avoiding oversharing addresses and verifying unknown links.
  • Think before posting photos or comments online because digital footprints last permanently.
  • Use a Bubble Map to describe online platforms or cyber citizens with precise adjectives: cautious, respectful, reliable, interactive.

Contoh: Write two sentences describing a responsible Internet user using adjectives from a Bubble Map.

Penyelesaian: A responsible Internet user is always cautious when opening unfamiliar attachments and remains respectful during online discussions.

Tip Penting: Bubble Maps work best when the outer bubbles hold adjectives rather than random sentences.

AMARAN PEMERIKSA #1

ELAKKAN: Keeping '-s' on the main verb after 'does/doesn't', e.g., 'Does Azmie knows the date?'

TEPAT: Drop the '-s' from the main verb: 'Does Azmie know the date?'

Tip Skor Exam: 'Does' already carries the third-person marker, so use the base verb after it.

AMARAN PEMERIKSA #2

ELAKKAN: Writing lowercase 'i' for the pronoun 'I' and text abbreviations like 'u' in exam emails.

TEPAT: Always capitalise 'I' and spell words in full ('you', 'for', 'to').

Tip Skor Exam: Treat every guided writing email as standard written English even when the tone is friendly.

AMARAN PEMERIKSA #3

ELAKKAN: Misplacing frequency adverbs after 'be', e.g., 'He always is polite online.'

TEPAT: Place frequency adverbs after 'am/is/are': 'He is always polite online.'

Tip Skor Exam: Before ordinary verbs ('always checks'), after 'be' ('is always careful').
