Objektif Pembelajaran DSKP

Sasaran Penguasaan Bab Ini

  1. Use Present Perfect Simple accurately with 'just' (very recently), 'already' (earlier than expected) and 'yet' (questions/negatives up to now).
  2. Contrast Present Perfect Simple (unfinished time/life experience) with Past Simple (finished time expressions like 'last year', 'yesterday', 'in 2022').
  3. Apply articles ('a', 'an', 'the' or zero article) accurately when describing cultural festivals, places and traditions.
  4. Use indefinite pronouns ('everyone', 'anyone', 'someone', 'no one', 'nothing', 'everything') with singular verbs.
Nota Ringkas & Contoh Terkawal (DSKP m.s. 40–45)

Ringkasan Teras Mengikut Subtopik

1.1 Present Perfect with 'just', 'already', 'yet', 'for' & 'since'

DSKP Form 3 builds directly on Form 2 by pairing Present Perfect Simple with five core time adverbs so students express recent actions and ongoing situations accurately.

  • 'just' (moments ago) and 'already' (sooner than expected) go BETWEEN 'have/has' and the Past Participle: 'We have just tasted traditional kuih.' / 'She has already finished her essay.'
  • 'yet' goes at the END of negative sentences and questions: 'Have they decorated the hall yet?' / 'No, they haven't arrived yet.'
  • 'for' expresses duration ('for three generations'), while 'since' marks the starting point ('since 1995').

Contoh Terkawal: Place 'already' or 'yet' correctly: 'Have you tried the Sarawak laksa ______? Yes, I have ______ had two bowls!'

Penyelesaian: First blank: 'yet' (end of question). Second blank: 'already' (between 'have' and 'had').

Tip Penting: Yet = End (Questions & Negatives); Just/Already = Middle (have + [word] + V3).

1.2 Present Perfect Simple vs. Past Simple

Choosing between Present Perfect and Past Simple is one of the highest-frequency B1 exam tasks in Form 3 UASA and SPM.

  • Use Present Perfect when the focus is on result or life experience with NO finished past time word ('Malaysia has hosted many cultural festivals').
  • Use Past Simple whenever a finished time marker appears ('ago', 'yesterday', 'last week', 'in 2018', 'when I was eight').
  • Notice the natural dialogue sequence: start broad with Present Perfect ('Have you ever visited Melaka?') → switch to Past Simple for details ('Yes, I went there last December').

Contoh Terkawal: Choose the correct tense: 'Our school (has celebrated / celebrated) Heritage Week last Friday, and the club (has raised / raised) over RM500 so far.'

Penyelesaian: 'celebrated' (finished time 'last Friday') and 'has raised' ('so far' connects up to the present).

Tip Penting: Spot the time anchor! 'Yesterday/last/in 2020' = Past Simple; 'so far/since/just/yet' = Present Perfect.

1.3 Articles ('a', 'an', 'the', Zero Article) for Culture & Places

Article accuracy separates B1 intermediate writing from A2 basics, especially when writing about Malaysian heritage, foods and institutions.

  • 'a / an': singular countable nouns mentioned for the first time ('a traditional instrument', 'an interesting custom').
  • 'the': specific items already known, unique items ('the Earth', 'the Yang di-Pertuan Agong'), superlatives ('the oldest temple') and some country/island groups ('the Philippines').
  • Zero article (no article): plural or uncountable nouns in general ('Tourists love Malaysian food'), most country names ('Malaysia', 'Japan'), languages ('Bahasa Melayu') and meals ('have breakfast').

Contoh Terkawal: Correct the article error: 'The Malaysian students enjoy playing the congkak during the recess at the school.'

Penyelesaian: Malaysian students enjoy playing congkak during recess at school.

Tip Penting: Drop 'the' before general plurals, named games/sports, meals and phrases like 'at school / at home'.

1.4 Indefinite Pronouns & Singular Verb Agreement

Indefinite pronouns refer to unspecified people, things or places in descriptions of family events and communities.

  • People: someone/somebody, anyone/anybody, everyone/everybody, no one/nobody. Things: something, anything, everything, nothing.
  • CRITICAL GRAMMAR RULE: Although 'everyone' and 'everybody' mean all people, they ALWAYS take a SINGULAR verb ('Everyone is welcome', 'Nobody knows').
  • Use 'some-' in positive sentences/offers ('Would you like something to eat?'), 'any-' in questions/negatives ('Did anyone bring a camera?'), and 'no-' with positive verbs ('No one forgot').

Contoh Terkawal: Choose the right verb: 'Everyone in the cultural choir (are / is) excited because nobody (have / has) missed a rehearsal.'

Penyelesaian: 'is' (singular after Everyone) and 'has' (singular after nobody).

Tip Penting: All words ending in -one, -body and -thing are grammatically SINGULAR!

Amaran Pemeriksa

3 Kesilapan Lazim & Tip Skor Exam

Kesilapan Lazim: Pairing Present Perfect with finished past markers like 'I have visited Ipoh last year.'

Pembetulan Tepat: Use Past Simple with finished time phrases: 'I visited Ipoh last year.'

Tip Skor Exam: Delete 'have/has' the moment you see 'yesterday', 'ago' or 'last ___'.

Kesilapan Lazim: Using plural verbs with 'everyone/everybody': 'Everybody have arrived for the cultural show.'

Pembetulan Tepat: Use singular 'has/is/was': 'Everybody has arrived for the cultural show.'

Tip Skor Exam: Even though 'everybody' feels plural in meaning, treat it like 'he/she' for verb grammar.

Kesilapan Lazim: Putting 'yet' in positive sentences like 'I have finished my project yet.'

Pembetulan Tepat: Use 'already' for positive completed actions ('I have already finished'), and save 'yet' for negatives and questions.

Tip Skor Exam: Already = Positive; Yet = Negative / Question.
