Ringkasan Teras Mengikut Subtopik

2.1 Countable/Uncountable Nouns & Quantifiers (Module 2 p. 21+)

Module 2 ('Ready for Anything') covers adventure stories, volunteering and preparedness, pairing noun types accurately with quantifiers.

  • Countable plural only: many, (a) few, several, a number of ('a few volunteers' = some positive; 'few volunteers' = almost none / negative).
  • Uncountable only: much, (a) little, a great deal of ('a little courage' = some; 'little time' = almost none).
  • Flexible (BOTH countable plural and uncountable): a lot of, lots of, plenty of, some, any, no, hardly any ('plenty of water' / 'plenty of volunteers').

Contoh Terkawal: Choose the best quantifier: 'Rescuers had (few / little) water left, yet (a few / a little) local hikers still offered help.'

Penyelesaian: 'little water' (uncountable, almost none) and 'a few local hikers' (plural countable, several helpful hikers).

Tip Penting: Positive tone ('enough to help') = 'a few / a little'; Negative tone ('nearly zero!') = 'few / little' without 'a'.

2.2 Defining vs. Non-Defining Relative Clauses

Relative clauses join short choppy sentences cleanly into complex SPM Band 4–5 sentences by defining people (who/that), things (which/that), places (where), possession (whose) and time (when).

  • DEFINING Relative Clause: gives ESSENTIAL information needed to identify the noun (NO commas!). Example: 'The firefighter who rescued the hiker won a medal.' Object pronoun ('The trail [that/which] we climbed was steep') can be omitted.
  • NON-DEFINING Relative Clause: gives EXTRA non-essential information about an already identified noun (MUST have commas!). Example: 'Mount Kinabalu, which is in Sabah, attracts climbers worldwide.'
  • IRON RULE of Non-Defining Clauses: NEVER use 'that' inside commas, and NEVER omit the relative pronoun ('My uncle, that lives in Ipoh...' is WRONG → use 'who').

Contoh Terkawal: Combine into ONE sentence with correct punctuation: 'Rani is an experienced paramedic. Her swift action saved two climbers.'

Penyelesaian: Rani, whose swift action saved two climbers, is an experienced paramedic. (Use commas + 'whose' for possession!)

Tip Penting: Commas present (Non-Defining) = Use 'who/which/whose/where', NEVER 'that'!

2.3 Past Simple vs. Past Continuous in Storytelling

Narrating exciting stories or emergencies ('Ready for Anything') relies on contrasting background ongoing actions with sudden interrupting events.

  • Past Continuous ('was/were + V-ing'): ongoing background scene or longer action interrupted midway ('The wind was howling'; 'While we were pitching the tent...').
  • Past Simple ('V2 / -ed'): finished sequence of actions or the sudden interrupting event ('...a branch fell onto the path').
  • Connectors: 'While / As' usually introduce the longer Past Continuous background action; 'When' commonly introduces the sudden Past Simple interruption.

Contoh Terkawal: Complete the emergency recount: 'While hikers ______ (cross) the wooden bridge, heavy rain suddenly ______ (begin).'

Penyelesaian: 'were crossing' (ongoing longer action after 'While') and 'began' (sudden interrupting action in Past Simple).

Tip Penting: Use Past Continuous for the movie background and Past Simple for the main plot actions!

2.4 Phrasal Verbs with 'keep' & Adjectives of Feeling

Module 2 enriches SPM narrative writing with vivid adjectives for emotions and multi-word verbs built around 'keep'.

  • Phrasal verbs with 'keep': 'keep up with' (stay at same pace/level), 'keep on (+ -ing)' (continue persistently), 'keep away from' (avoid danger), 'keep in touch with' (maintain contact), 'keep an eye on' (watch carefully).
  • Adjective pairs (-ed for feelings vs -ing for things): 'exhausted' (how hikers felt) vs 'exhausting' (describing the hike); 'terrified' vs 'terrifying'; 'relieved' vs 'relieving'; 'thrilled' vs 'thrilling'.
  • Always use strong action verbs and emotional reactions when drafting Part 3 SPM narrative stories.

Contoh Terkawal: Fix the adjective error: 'After walking 15 km uphill, the scouts were very exhausting, yet they kept to walk until camp.'

Penyelesaian: After walking 15 km uphill, the scouts were very exhausted, yet they kept on walking until camp.

Tip Penting: People FEEL '-ed' (exhausted, relieved); situations ARE '-ing' (exhausting, thrilling); 'keep on' + V-ing!

Amaran Pemeriksa

3 Kesilapan Lazim & Tip Skor Exam

Kesilapan Lazim: Writing 'that' inside a non-defining relative clause with commas: 'Kuala Lumpur, that is Malaysia's capital, has tall towers.'

Pembetulan Tepat: Replace 'that' with 'which': 'Kuala Lumpur, which is Malaysia's capital, has tall towers.'

Tip Skor Exam: See commas around a relative clause? Ban the word 'that'!

Kesilapan Lazim: Using 'who's' instead of 'whose' for possession in relative clauses ('The student who's bag was lost').

Pembetulan Tepat: 'Who's' means 'who is / who has'; possession MUST be spelled 'whose' ('The student whose bag was lost').

Tip Skor Exam: Test by expanding: does 'who is bag' make sense? No → use 'whose'!

Kesilapan Lazim: Using Past Continuous for every action in a list ('I was waking up, was brushing my teeth and was walking out').

Pembetulan Tepat: Sequential completed actions in a narrative use Past Simple: 'I woke up, brushed my teeth and walked out.'

Tip Skor Exam: Past Simple = Action 1 → Action 2 → Action 3; Past Continuous = background setting or interrupted action.

Which sentence uses a Non-Defining Relative Clause correctly?

Huraian: Non-defining clauses require opening AND closing commas and never use 'that' (Option A uses 'that'; C/D omit the closing comma; D also misuses 'where' instead of 'which' as the object of 'climbed').

Soalan 2 / 10 +10 XP

Complete with the accurate pronoun: 'That is the volunteer ______ first-aid kit helped the injured cyclist.'

Huraian: Possessive relative pronoun showing ownership ('his/her first-aid kit') is 'whose'.

Soalan 3 / 10 +10 XP

Select the correct quantifier sentence.

Huraian: 'Water' is uncountable and pairs with 'much' and 'a little'; 'time' needs 'little', and 'volunteers/lanterns' are countable.

Soalan 4 / 10 +10 XP

Choose the right narrative tense pair: 'As the lifeguard ______ (watch) the waves, he ______ (spot) a boy waving for help.'

Huraian: Background ongoing action ('was watching') interrupted by a sudden sighting ('spotted').

Soalan 5 / 10 +10 XP

What does the phrasal verb 'keep up with' mean in: 'I walked fast to keep up with the experienced jungle guide.'?

Huraian: 'Keep up with' means moving or progressing at the same rate so you don't fall behind.

Soalan 6 / 10 +10 XP

Which sentence describes a person's feeling accurately?

Huraian: People experience '-ed' emotions ('relieved', 'thrilled', 'surprised'); events are '-ing' ('exhausting').

Soalan 7 / 10 +10 XP

Identify the sentence where the relative pronoun CAN be omitted safely.

Huraian: In 'The compass [that] I borrowed...', 'that' is the OBJECT of a defining clause ('I borrowed it'), so it can be omitted ('The compass I borrowed...').

Soalan 8 / 10 +10 XP

Complete cleanly: 'Despite heavy rain, the search team kept ______ looking until dusk.'

Huraian: 'keep on + V-ing' means to continue doing something persistently.

Soalan 9 / 10 +10 XP

Choose the correct contrast between 'few' and 'a few'.

Huraian: 'few' without 'a' carries a negative sense of scarcity; 'a few' indicates a positive small number.

Soalan 10 / 10 +10 XP

Combine properly: 'We visited a village. My grandmother grew up in that village.'

Huraian: 'in that village' (location adverbial) combines using 'where' ('in which').
