SUBTOPIK 01

6.1 Passive Voice Across Tenses & Two-Object Verbs

Module 6 covers how news, game reviews, and event notices shift focus onto actions and recipients using the Passive Voice.

  • Core Formula: Subject + tense of 'be' + Past Participle (V3). Continuous tenses require 'being + V3' ('The martial arts moves are being demonstrated right now'; 'The pitch was being mown'). Present Perfect Passive uses 'has/have BEEN + V3' (NEVER 'has being').
  • Two-Object Verbs (give, offer, send, show, tell): 'They offered Tracy a role' -> (1) 'Tracy was offered a role' OR (2) 'A role was offered TO Tracy'.
  • Passive 'make' Trap (p. 145): Active 'The coach made us practise' drops 'to', whereas Passive brings 'to' back: 'We were made TO practise!'

Contoh: Convert into TWO passive forms: 'The tournament judges awarded Aiman the Best Gamer trophy.'

Penyelesaian: (1) Aiman was awarded the Best Gamer trophy (by the judges). (2) The Best Gamer trophy was awarded TO Aiman.

Tip Penting: Spot continuous '-ing' -> use 'being + V3'; put the thing first -> add 'to' before the person!

SUBTOPIK 02

6.2 Clauses of Result, Concession & Purpose

Explaining leisure habits, gaming pros/cons, and rules requires exact linker grammar after 'so/such', 'despite/although', and 'so that/in order to'.

  • Result: 'so + adj/adv + (that)' ('so gripping that...') vs 'such + (a/an) + adj + NOUN + (that)' ('such a talented opponent that...'). Also pair 'so much/many' against 'such a lot of'!
  • Concession: 'although / even though + Subject + Verb' vs 'despite / in spite of + NOUN / V-ing / the fact that...'. IRON RULE: NEVER write 'despite of'!
  • Purpose: 'in order (not) to / so as (not) to + V1' vs 'so that + Subject + can/could/will/would/may/might + V1' ('I booked early so that I could get front seats').

Contoh: Rewrite using 'despite' AND 'so much': (1) 'Although it rained heavily, the match continued.' (2) 'There is such a lot of noise that I can't concentrate.'

Penyelesaian: (1) Despite the heavy rain (or Despite raining heavily), the match continued. (2) There is so much noise that I can't concentrate.

Tip Penting: 'Despite' = NO 'of', NO bare clause; 'although' = full Subject + Verb!

SUBTOPIK 03

6.3 'Over-' Verbs, Word Building (-ness/-ity) & Gaming Lexis

Module 6 strengthens vocabulary precision through 'over-' verb distinctions, noun suffixes, and fixed prepositions.

  • 'over-' verbs: 'overlook' (fail to notice / ignore), 'overhear' (hear accidentally), 'overtake' (pass someone ahead), 'overcome' (defeat an obstacle), 'oversleep' (sleep past your alarm).
  • Abstract nouns (-ness / -ity): kind -> kindness, dark -> darkness, popular -> popularity, able -> ability, creative -> creativity.
  • Fixed idioms & prepositions (p. 145): 'prevent someone FROM doing something', 'take it FOR granted', 'wind someone up' (annoy / get on someone's nerves), 'hook up with' (meet/connect).

Contoh: Fill in the accurate verb and preposition: 'Critics often ______ (overlook/overtake) how strategy games build teenagers' ______ (CREATE), yet teens shouldn't be prevented ______ studying.'

Penyelesaian: 'overlook', 'creativity', and 'from'.

Tip Penting: 'Prevent + object + FROM + -ing' and 'take something FOR granted' are top Paper 1 Cloze items!

SUBTOPIK 04

6.4 Indirect Questions & Film / Game Reviews

Module 6 Writing develops polite semi-formal enquiry emails using indirect questions alongside engaging spoiler-free film or game reviews.

  • Indirect Questions: 'Could you tell me / I would like to know + Wh-/if + Subject + Verb'. Drop 'do/does/did' ('Could you tell me what time the cinema opens?', NOT 'what time does the cinema open?').
  • End Punctuation Check: 'Could you tell me...?' ends with '?', while 'I would like to know...' ends with a full stop '.'.
  • Film/Game Review Blueprint: (1) Title, genre & director/studio, (2) Brief plot setup in PRESENT SIMPLE without spoilers, (3) Specific evaluation (soundtrack, acting, pacing) using vivid adjectives ('gripping, believable, predictable, disappointing'), (4) Target audience recommendation.

Contoh: Convert into a polite indirect question: 'How much does a weekend arcade pass cost?'

Penyelesaian: Could you tell me how much a weekend arcade pass costs? (Drop 'does' and add '-s' to 'cost' in statement order!)

Tip Penting: Indirect questions keep affirmative word order and review plot summaries stay in Present Simple!

AMARAN PEMERIKSA #1

ELAKKAN: Writing 'despite of' or putting a full clause directly after 'despite' ('Despite of the storm' / 'Despite he practised hard').

TEPAT: Never add 'of' after 'despite', and follow 'despite / in spite of' with a noun, '-ing', or 'the fact that': 'Despite the storm' / 'Despite practising hard'.

Tip Skor Exam: In spite OF (3 words) vs Despite (1 word, NO 'of')!

AMARAN PEMERIKSA #2

ELAKKAN: Leaving 'do/does/did' inside an indirect question ('Could you tell me where does the exhibition take place?').

TEPAT: Remove 'do/does/did' and use normal statement word order: 'Could you tell me where the exhibition takes place?'

Tip Skor Exam: Indirect question = Wh-/if + Subject + Verb!

AMARAN PEMERIKSA #3

ELAKKAN: Dropping 'to' in the passive of 'make' ('We were made clean the sports hall') or writing 'has being proved'.

TEPAT: Passive 'be made' requires 'to + V1' ('We were made to clean'), and Present Perfect Passive uses 'has been proved'.

Tip Skor Exam: Active 'made us clean' -> Passive 'were made TO clean'; Perfect Passive = 'has BEEN V3'!

Soalan 1 / 20 +10 XP

Convert into Present Continuous Passive: 'The coach is showing the beginners new badminton footwork right now.'

Huraian: Present Continuous Active ('is showing') becomes Present Continuous Passive ('are being shown').

Soalan 2 / 20 +10 XP

Complete the Passive transformation: Active: 'Our teacher made us rewrite the review.' -> Passive: 'We were made ______ the review.'

Huraian: Although active 'make + object + V1' uses a bare infinitive, the passive form 'be made' always takes 'to + V1'.

Soalan 3 / 20 +10 XP

Choose the correct alternative passive of 'The festival organisers sent every volunteer a free concert pass.'

Huraian: When the direct object ('a free concert pass') becomes the passive subject, the recipient takes 'to' ('sent to every volunteer').

Soalan 4 / 20 +10 XP

Select the accurate Present Perfect Passive: 'Studies show it ______ proved that cooperative games improve problem-solving.'

Huraian: Present Perfect Passive is formed with 'has/have + been + V3' ('has been proved').

Soalan 5 / 20 +10 XP

Choose the accurate Result structure: 'Gary faced ______ a skilled chess opponent that the final lasted three hours.'

Huraian: Before a singular countable noun phrase ('a skilled chess opponent'), use 'such' ('such a ... that').

Soalan 6 / 20 +10 XP

Rewrite accurately: 'There is such a lot of background noise in the hall that we cannot hear the speaker.'

Huraian: 'Such a lot of + uncountable noun (noise)' transforms into 'so much + uncountable noun'.

Soalan 7 / 20 +10 XP

Complete the Concession sentence: '______ arriving twenty minutes late, our team still won the robotics showcase.'

Huraian: Followed by a gerund ('arriving'), use 'In spite of' or 'Despite' (never 'Despite of' or 'Although' without Subject + Verb).

Soalan 8 / 20 +10 XP

Choose the Purpose structure that fits before 'find out': 'Players often read historical notes ______ find out more about the setting.'

Huraian: Before a base verb ('find out'), use 'in order to' or 'so as to'; 'so that' requires a Subject + Modal clause.

Soalan 9 / 20 +10 XP

Which 'over-' verb means 'fail to notice or ignore something important'?

Huraian: 'Overlook' means to fail to notice or ignore (e.g., overlooking positive benefits of teamwork).

Soalan 10 / 20 +10 XP

Complete the fixed preposition: 'Strict curfews should not prevent teenagers ______ enjoying healthy outdoor sports on weekends.'

Huraian: 'Prevent somebody FROM doing something' always takes 'from' + '-ing'.

Soalan 11 / 20 +10 XP

Form the correct abstract noun from [POPULAR]: 'The rapid rise in e-sports ______ surprised many local broadcasters.'

Huraian: 'Popular' forms the abstract noun 'popularity' using the suffix '-ity'.

Soalan 12 / 20 +10 XP

What does the expression 'wind someone up' mean in Module 6?

Huraian: 'Wind somebody up' means to annoy or tease them intentionally.

Soalan 13 / 20 +10 XP

Convert 'What time does the pottery workshop begin?' into a polite indirect question.

Huraian: Remove 'does', transfer '-s' onto 'begins', and place Subject before Verb ('the pottery workshop begins').

Soalan 14 / 20 +10 XP

Which indirect question punctuation is 100% accurate?

Huraian: 'I would like to know...' is a declarative statement ending with a full stop '.', whereas 'Could you tell me...?' ends with '?'.

Soalan 15 / 20 +10 XP

Which sentence follows SPM Film Review conventions cleanly?

Huraian: Reviews outline the premise in Present Simple without spoiling the ending and maintain vivid semi-formal register.

Soalan 16 / 20 +10 XP

Choose the strongest evaluative adjectives for a thrilling mystery game review: 'With its ______ storyline and ______ sound design, every chapter keeps players guessing.'

Huraian: 'Gripping' (exciting/holding attention) and 'atmospheric' replace vague 'good/nice' praise in Module 6 reviews.

Soalan 17 / 20 +10 XP

Complete the idiom: 'Many teenagers ______ for granted how fast modern fibre broadband streams high-definition films.'

Huraian: The fixed Module 6 idiom is 'take it for granted (that...)'.

Soalan 18 / 20 +10 XP

Rewrite using 'even though': 'We enjoyed the outdoor concert despite the muddy field.'

Huraian: 'Even though' must introduce a full Subject + Verb clause ('the field was muddy').

Soalan 19 / 20 +10 XP

Spot the sentence with ZERO errors in Impersonal Passive reporting: 'People say that regular chess sharpens memory.'

Huraian: Personal/Impersonal passive uses 'Subject + is said + to + V1' ('Regular chess is said to sharpen memory') without 'that'.

Soalan 20 / 20 +10 XP

Complete accurately: 'I ______ (oversleep) because I forgot to charge my alarm clock, so the rival runner ______ (overtake) me near the finish line.'

Huraian: 'Oversleep' = sleep too long past an alarm; 'overtake' = pass another runner or vehicle moving in the same direction.
