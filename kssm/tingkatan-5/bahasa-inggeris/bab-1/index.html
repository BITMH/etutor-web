Sasaran Penguasaan Bab Ini

  1. Differentiate modern family structures (traditional, extended, blended, adoptive, single-parent) and phrasal verbs of relationships ('get on with', 'fall out', 'look up to', 'get together').
  2. Distinguish dynamic vs. stative uses of verbs like 'have', 'think', 'see', 'appear' and 'taste' in Present Simple vs. Present Continuous.
  3. Construct Defining and Non-Defining Relative Clauses cleanly (recognising when object pronouns can be omitted and never using 'that' after commas).
  4. Apply formal and informal preposition placement in relative clauses ('the chore that I hate' vs 'the relative to whom I spoke').
Nota Ringkas & Contoh Terkawal (pp. 1–12)

Ringkasan Teras Mengikut Subtopik

1.1 Family Types & Household Relationships (Unit 1 Reading)

Unit 1 ('It's Personal!') explores changing family structures, fair household chore sharing and maintaining healthy ties with relatives and peers.

  • Family vocabulary (p. 5): traditional/nuclear family (parents & children), extended family (grandparents, aunts, uncles under one roof or close ties), blended family (stepparents/stepsiblings after remarriage), adoptive family, single-parent family.
  • Relationship collocations & phrasal verbs: 'share the same interests', 'get together with relatives', 'go on strike' (stop working in protest), 'pull your weight' (do your fair share of chores), 'get on well with' (be friendly), 'look up to' (admire).
  • In the reading article, household chores historically fell unevenly on women, whereas modern fairness expects every member to lend a hand.

Contoh Terkawal: Identify the family type: 'When Amir's father remarried, Amir gained a stepmother and two younger stepsisters living in the same house.'

Penyelesaian: Blended family (formed when one or both partners bring children from a previous marriage into a new family household).

Tip Penting: Always separate 'extended family' (multigenerational aunts/grandparents) from 'blended family' (stepparents/stepsiblings).

1.2 Present Simple, Present Continuous & Meaning-Shift Verbs

Beyond basic routines vs. temporary actions, Form 5 tests verbs that CAN take '-ing' ONLY when their meaning shifts from a permanent state to an active behaviour or planned event.

  • THINK: 'I think family loyalty matters' (opinion = stative) vs. 'We are thinking of organising a family dinner' (considering = dynamic).
  • HAVE: 'They have a huge house' (possession = stative) vs. 'We are having a huge get-together on Sunday' (hosting/experiencing event = dynamic).
  • SEE / LOOK / APPEAR / TASTE: 'She looks tired' (seems = stative) vs. 'He is looking at old family albums' (action); 'My brother is always leaving wet towels on the sofa!' (annoying repeated habit uses Present Continuous + 'always').

Contoh Terkawal: Explain the meaning difference: (1) 'Aunt Maya has three children.' vs (2) 'Aunt Maya is having lunch with us now.'

Penyelesaian: (1) 'has' shows permanent family possession (stative). (2) 'is having' describes the ongoing action of eating a meal right now (dynamic).

Tip Penting: Notice Present Continuous + 'always/constantly' expresses mild irritation about a bad household habit!

1.3 Relative Clauses & Pronoun Omission Rules (Grammar 1.4–1.7)

Download Unit 1 revisits Defining and Non-Defining Relative Clauses with sharper Form 5 precision regarding object pronoun omission.

  • Defining Relative Clause (no commas, essential meaning): 'The chore which/that I hate most is washing-up.' Because 'which/that' is the OBJECT of 'I hate', it CAN be omitted → 'The chore I hate most is washing-up.'
  • NEVER omit a SUBJECT relative pronoun: 'The sister who helps Mum is Sarah.' (Wrong: 'The sister helps Mum is Sarah').
  • Non-Defining Relative Clause (extra info inside commas): 'My eldest cousin, who studies medicine in Penang, visits us every Eid.' NEVER use 'that' and NEVER omit the relative pronoun inside commas!

Contoh Terkawal: Decide if 'who/that' can be deleted safely: (A) 'The neighbour who lives next door babysits for us.' (B) 'The cousin who we met in Melaka works as a chef.'

Penyelesaian: (A) CANNOT be omitted because 'who' is the subject of 'lives'. (B) CAN be omitted ('The cousin we met in Melaka...') because 'we' is the subject and 'who' is the object of 'met'!

Tip Penting: Quick omission test: Look immediately AFTER who/which/that — is there a new noun/pronoun subject ('I/we/they')? Yes = object pronoun (omissible in defining clauses)!

1.4 Prepositions in Relative Clauses & Email Paragraphing

Form 5 introduces formal vs. everyday preposition placement in relative clauses plus paragraph organisation for SPM Part 1 email writing.

  • Everyday/neutral style places the preposition at the END of the clause (allowing 'that' or omission): 'The relative (who/that) I rely on most is my uncle.'
  • Formal style places the preposition BEFORE 'whom' (people) or 'which' (things) — NEVER before 'who' or 'that', and you CANNOT omit 'whom/which': 'The relative on whom I rely most is my uncle.' / 'The chore about which he complained...'
  • Email structure (p. 11): use a separate paragraph for each new topic so readers follow smoothly, with clear topic transitions.

Contoh Terkawal: Rewrite formally by moving the preposition: 'The aunt who I talked to gave me helpful advice.'

Penyelesaian: The aunt to whom I talked gave me helpful advice. (Preposition before a person pronoun MUST use 'whom', NEVER 'to who' or 'to that'!)

Tip Penting: Preposition + WHOM (person) / WHICH (thing) — never 'preposition + who' or 'preposition + that'!

Amaran Pemeriksa

3 Kesilapan Lazim & Tip Skor Exam

Kesilapan Lazim: Writing 'to who' or 'for that' when placing a preposition before a relative pronoun ('The friend with who I live').

Pembetulan Tepat: After a preposition, change 'who' to 'whom' and never use 'that': 'The friend with whom I live' (or informal: 'The friend I live with').

Tip Skor Exam: Preposition upfront? Only WHOM or WHICH survive!

Kesilapan Lazim: Treating all uses of 'have' or 'think' as strictly forbidden in '-ing' form ('We have a party this Sunday' for an arranged plan).

Pembetulan Tepat: Use Present Continuous when 'have' means 'host/eat/enjoy' ('We are having a family barbecue on Sunday') or 'think' means 'consider' ('I'm thinking of apologising').

Tip Skor Exam: Does 'have' mean 'own/possess'? Keep Simple. Does it mean 'eat/celebrate/experience'? Use '-ing'!

Kesilapan Lazim: Omitting the pronoun in a non-defining relative clause ('My grandmother, I admire very much, lives in Kedah').

Pembetulan Tepat: Never omit pronouns inside non-defining clauses with commas: 'My grandmother, whom/who I admire very much, lives in Kedah.'

Tip Skor Exam: Commas present = KEEP the relative pronoun and NEVER use 'that'.

Soalan 1 / 10 +10 XP

Which family type consists of parents, children, grandparents, aunts and uncles maintaining close daily contact or living together?

Huraian: An extended family includes relatives beyond parents and children, such as grandparents, aunts, uncles and cousins.

Soalan 2 / 10 +10 XP

Choose the sentence where 'think' uses the continuous form accurately.

Huraian: Mental consideration ('thinking about solving') is an active process, whereas opinions ('think that...') stay Simple.

Soalan 3 / 10 +10 XP

Select the grammatically CORRECT formal relative clause with a preposition upfront.

Huraian: A preposition placed before a person relative pronoun requires 'whom' ('to whom I wrote') without duplicating 'to' at the end.

Soalan 4 / 10 +10 XP

In which defining clause CAN the relative pronoun safely be omitted?

Huraian: 'that' is the object of 'our parents assign', and the clause is defining (no commas), so 'The chores our parents assign...' is grammatical.

Soalan 5 / 10 +10 XP

Which sentence expresses irritation about someone's repeated annoying household habit?

Huraian: Present Continuous + 'always/forever/constantly' conveys annoyance at a repeated habit.

Soalan 6 / 10 +10 XP

Complete cleanly: 'Our family ______ a huge reunion dinner for all thirty relatives next Sunday evening.'

Huraian: Singular collective 'family' + arranged near-future celebration ('have' = host/enjoy) uses 'is having'.

Soalan 7 / 10 +10 XP

Identify the sentence with ZERO errors in relative clause punctuation.

Huraian: Non-defining clauses need both opening and closing commas and must use 'who/which', never 'that'.

Soalan 8 / 10 +10 XP

What does the expression 'pull your weight' mean in Unit 1?

Huraian: 'Pull your weight' means contributing your fair share of work alongside other family or team members.

Soalan 9 / 10 +10 XP

Choose the right meaning shift: 'The soup ______ delicious!' vs 'The chef ______ the soup right now.'

Huraian: Stative quality ('tastes delicious' = has a flavour) vs deliberate physical action ('is tasting' = sampling with a spoon).

Soalan 10 / 10 +10 XP

Why should a formal or friendly email separate each new topic into a new paragraph (Unit 1 p. 11)?

Huraian: Clear paragraphing by topic improves readability and scores higher for Organisation in SPM Writing.
