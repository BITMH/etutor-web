Nota Ringkas & Contoh Terkawal (pp. 13–24)

Ringkasan Teras Mengikut Subtopik

2.1 Past Simple vs. Past Continuous in Mystery Stories

Narrating unexplained events such as the Winchester Mystery House or strange disappearances requires precise control over completed events vs. background atmosphere.

  • Past Simple: single completed actions in sequence ('In 1884, Sarah Winchester left Connecticut, travelled to California and bought a farmhouse'), historical facts and permanent past states.
  • Past Continuous ('was/were + V-ing'): ongoing scene-setting ('Moonlight was shining through the attic window'), two parallel ongoing actions ('While builders were hammering upstairs, Sarah was sketching new blueprints'), or interrupted actions.
  • Time anchors: 'over the next thirty-eight years', 'while', 'as', 'suddenly', 'when', 'at that moment'.

Contoh Terkawal: Complete the narrative: 'While carpenters ______ (build) staircases that led nowhere, rumours quickly ______ (spread) across the town.'

Penyelesaian: 'were building' (ongoing activity over a period) and 'spread' (verb 'spread' has irregular Past Simple 'spread', no '-ed'!).

Tip Penting: Watch irregular verbs like spread-spread-spread, cost-cost-cost and put-put-put!

2.2 'Used to' vs. 'Would' for Past Habits and States

This is one of the highest-yield grammar contrasts in Form 5 SPM. Both describe past routines no longer true today, YET they obey one strict restriction regarding STATIVE verbs.

  • 'used to + base verb': works for BOTH past repeated actions AND past states/situations ('Sarah used to sleep in a different bedroom every night'; 'She used to own a huge fortune').
  • 'would + base verb': works ONLY for REPEATED PAST ACTIONS / habits ('She would wear one of 13 special coloured robes and ask spirits for guidance').
  • CRITICAL EXAM RULE: NEVER use 'would' with stative verbs ('live', 'be', 'own', 'have', 'like', 'know', 'believe') for past states! Say 'She used to live in Connecticut', NEVER 'She would live in Connecticut'.

Contoh Terkawal: Decide whether 'would' is valid in both sentences: (1) 'My grandad used to tell ghost stories by lantern light.' (2) 'That old mansion used to belong to a wealthy inventor.'

Penyelesaian: (1) 'tell' is a repeated past action → 'would tell' IS VALID! (2) 'belong' is a past state → 'would belong' is INVALID; ONLY 'used to belong' is grammatical!

Tip Penting: Past ACTION repeated regularly = used to OR would; Past STATE (be/live/have/belong/believe) = ONLY used to!

2.3 Reading Mystery: Winchester Mystery House (p. 18–19)

Unit 2 features the real-life history of Sarah Winchester, whose grief and eccentric architectural habits sparked a century-old legend in California.

  • Background: After losing her child and husband (inheriting wealth from the Winchester rifle company), heartbroken Sarah left Connecticut in 1884 for California.
  • Mystery construction: For 38 years she employed builders round-the-clock adding strange rooms, doors opening into walls and staircases leading nowhere.
  • Habits & beliefs: Legend says she slept in a different bedroom every two nights and consulted robes/seances to confuse spirits — reminding readers how stories blend facts with legend.

Contoh Terkawal: Why did Sarah Winchester allegedly never sleep in the same bedroom two nights in a row?

Penyelesaian: According to the reading text, she wanted to confuse spirits she believed were haunting her home.

Tip Penting: When answering SPM comprehension questions, distinguish verified biographical dates (1884, 38 years) from reported beliefs ('Apparently...').

2.4 Past Deduction & Narrative Tension Builders

When debating unsolved mysteries (like UFOs, Bermuda Triangle or ancient ruins), Unit 2 pairs past tenses with speculation vocabulary.

  • Phrases for explaining mysteries: 'archaeologists believe', 'there is no logical explanation', 'it must have been...', 'it could/might have been...', 'it can't have been...' (impossible).
  • Suspicious/mysterious vocab: bizarre, eerie, vanish without a trace, hoax, witness, legend, rumour, clue, investigate.
  • Dramatic story openings: avoid dull 'One day I went...' — start with sensory sound, unexpected weather or a puzzling discovery.

Contoh Terkawal: Respond using a modal of deduction: 'The locked attic door swung open with nobody upstairs and muddy footprints outside.'

Penyelesaian: Someone must have entered earlier, or the wind might have blown a loose latch open — it can't have been an animal wearing boots!

Tip Penting: 'Must have V3' = almost 100% sure; 'Might/Could have V3' = possible; 'Can't have V3' = impossible (NEVER 'mustn't have' for deduction!).

Amaran Pemeriksa

3 Kesilapan Lazim & Tip Skor Exam

Kesilapan Lazim: Using 'would' for past states, e.g. 'Years ago, there would be a creepy well behind our school.'

Pembetulan Tepat: 'Be' describes a state, not a repeated action, so use 'used to': 'There used to be a creepy well...'.

Tip Skor Exam: Ask: Could you actually SEE someone doing that action repeatedly every evening? If no (like 'be/live/have/believe'), ban 'would'!

Kesilapan Lazim: Writing 'She didn't used to believe in legends' or 'Did people would tell stories?'

Pembetulan Tepat: After 'didn't/did', drop '-d': 'didn't use to believe'. And NEVER form yes/no questions about past habits with 'Did ... would' — use 'Did people use to tell...?'

Tip Skor Exam: Negatives and questions belong to 'use to', almost never 'would'!

Kesilapan Lazim: Using 'mustn't have' as the opposite of 'must have' when deducing mysteries ('It mustn't have been a ghost').

Pembetulan Tepat: The logical opposite of certainty 'must have been' is 'can't have been' ('It can't have been a ghost; I saw the curtain string').

Tip Skor Exam: Sure YES = must have; Sure NO = can't have!

In which sentence can 'used to' be replaced with 'would' WITHOUT a grammar error?

Huraian: 'gather' and 'tell' are repeated past actions ('would gather... tell'); 'live', 'own' and 'believe' are past states where 'would' cannot be used.

Soalan 2 / 10 +10 XP

Choose the sentence from Unit 2 style that uses 'would' incorrectly.

Huraian: 'have' (possess a fortune) is a stative verb; it must be 'Sarah used to have...'.

Soalan 3 / 10 +10 XP

According to the reading text in Unit 2, what unusual construction pattern happened at Winchester House starting in 1884?

Huraian: For 38 years starting in 1884 in California, builders worked continuously expanding the unfinished farmhouse.

Soalan 4 / 10 +10 XP

Select the accurate Past Simple + Past Continuous sentence.

Huraian: Background ongoing 'were examining' + sudden interruption 'fell out' is accurate (Option B has 'was fall', C uses Continuous for quick sequence, D keeps 'saw' after 'Did').

Soalan 5 / 10 +10 XP

Which negative sentence about past habits is grammatically accurate?

Huraian: 'didn't + use to + base verb' drops the '-d' after 'didn't'.

Soalan 6 / 10 +10 XP

'Look at those giant circular tracks — that's impossible for a bicycle! Someone ______ driven a tractor onto the pitch last night.'

Huraian: 'must have + V3' expresses strong logical certainty about a past event based on evidence.

Soalan 7 / 10 +10 XP

Which word best fits: 'The old lighthouse keeper disappeared completely; nobody found a single ______ as to what happened.'

Huraian: A 'clue' is a piece of evidence helping solve a mystery.

Soalan 8 / 10 +10 XP

Complete cleanly: 'At midnight, while rain ______ against the attic roof, Mia thought she heard footsteps.'

Huraian: Ongoing atmospheric background in the past after 'while' uses Past Continuous ('was beating').

Soalan 9 / 10 +10 XP

Which deduction expresses IMPOSSIBILITY about a past mystery?

Huraian: Impossibility in the past is expressed with 'can't have + V3' (or 'couldn't have').
