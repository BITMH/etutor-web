SUBTOPIK 01

6.1 Offenders, Crimes & Justice Vocabulary

Unit 6 sharpens vocabulary precision by matching specific crime labels to method, target, and exact verb-object pairings.

  • Offender Nouns: 'burglar' (breaks into a building to steal), 'robber' (steals using force or threat from a bank/person), 'shoplifter' (steals items displayed in a store), 'arsonist' (deliberately sets fires), 'vandal' (intentionally damages property), 'trespasser' (enters private land without permission).
  • 'Rob' vs 'Steal': You ROB a bank, shop, or person; you STEAL money, jewellery, or watches!
  • Crime Expressions: 'break into' (force entry), 'set off the alarm' (trigger the security siren), 'do time' (serve a prison sentence), 'be under arrest'.

Contoh: Fix both word errors: 'A trespasser robbed three diamond watches after breaking the museum alarm off.'

Penyelesaian: A burglar stole three diamond watches after setting off the museum alarm.

Tip Penting: ROB a place/person, STEAL the goods; 'SET OFF the alarm', 'BREAK INTO a building'!

SUBTOPIK 02

6.2 Ability, Permission & Necessity Modals

Unit 6 highlights two major past modal traps frequently tested in SPM Paper 1 Part 2 and Part 3.

  • Past Ability ('was/were able to' vs 'could'): General past ability uses 'could' ('She could run fast at school'), BUT a SINGLE successful past achievement MUST use 'was/were able to' or 'managed to' ('Police were able to trace the van yesterday' — NEVER affirmative 'could'!). Negative past accepts BOTH ('couldn't / wasn't able to').
  • 'didn't need to + V1' vs 'needn't have + V3': 'didn't need to' = unnecessary (usually skipped). 'needn't have + V3' = unnecessary, YET someone wasted effort doing it anyway! ('Detective Lim needn't have printed 50 photos — the case had already closed!').
  • Present Obligation contrast: 'mustn't' (forbidden) vs 'don't have to' (optional).

Contoh: Choose the right forms: 'Officers ______ (were able to / could) catch the thief at the toll gate, though they ______ (didn't need to / needn't have) bring dogs because he surrendered immediately — oops, they brought them anyway!'

Penyelesaian: 'were able to' (single past success) and 'needn't have brought' (done needlessly).

Tip Penting: One-off past success = was/were able to; Wasted completed action = needn't have + V3!

SUBTOPIK 03

6.3 Past Modals of Deduction & Hindsight Criticism

Interpreting forensic clues and alibis requires matching certainty levels precisely to past modal auxiliaries.

  • Near Certainty (True): 'must have + V3' ('No lock scratches — the thief must have used a spare key').
  • Near Certainty (Impossible): 'can't / couldn't have + V3' ('She was live on TV in Penang, so she can't / couldn't have robbed the Johor bank'). NEVER use 'mustn't have' for impossibility!
  • Weak Possibility: 'may / might / could + have + V3'. Watch negative possibility: use 'may not / might not have + V3' (NEVER 'couldn't have' for 'maybe not', because 'couldn't have' means 'impossible'!).
  • Hindsight Criticism / Regret: 'should (not) / ought (not) to have + V3' ('You shouldn't have left the safe unlocked').

Contoh: Fix the deduction trap: 'He mustn't have broken into the office because his keycard was in Penang!'

Penyelesaian: He can't (or couldn't) have broken into the office because his keycard was in Penang!

Tip Penting: Alibi/impossible = can't/couldn't have; Doubtful 'maybe not' = might not have; Mistake = shouldn't have!

SUBTOPIK 04

6.4 Persuasive Crime Essays & Cause-Effect Arguments

Unit 6 Writing guides students to replace emotional generalisations with objective cause-and-effect arguments and preventive solutions.

  • Objective Framing: replace 'I think criminals are bad people' with analytical evidence ('Poor street lighting increases vulnerability to opportunistic theft').
  • Two-Pronged Solutions: balance short-term deterrence (bright streetlights, CCTV, neighbourhood patrols) with long-term root prevention (youth workshops, digital awareness, parental mentorship).
  • Formal Transitions: 'Consequently', 'Moreover', 'Furthermore', 'In addition to stricter enforcement'.

Contoh: Upgrade this informal essay line: 'Anyway, cops are bad at stopping vandals because teens are bored.'

Penyelesaian: 'Furthermore, expanding constructive youth workshops addresses the boredom and lack of guidance that often underlie petty vandalism.'

Tip Penting: Combine short-term deterrence with root-cause prevention using formal transitions like 'Furthermore' and 'Consequently'!

AMARAN PEMERIKSA #1

ELAKKAN: Using affirmative 'could' for a single past success ('Luckily, police could arrest the suspect last night').

TEPAT: Use 'were able to' or 'managed to' for a specific one-off past achievement: 'Luckily, police were able to arrest the suspect last night.'

Tip Skor Exam: General skill = could; Single past victory = was/were able to!

AMARAN PEMERIKSA #2

ELAKKAN: Writing 'mustn't have + V3' for past impossibility or mixing up 'rob' and 'steal' ('robbed RM500').

TEPAT: Use 'can't / couldn't have + V3' for impossibility, and 'steal' + money/goods vs 'rob' + bank/shop/person.

Tip Skor Exam: Impossible = can't have; ROB a bank, STEAL cash!

AMARAN PEMERIKSA #3

ELAKKAN: Confusing 'didn't need to' with 'needn't have + V3' when an unnecessary action WAS actually performed.

TEPAT: When someone already did the unnecessary task ('What a waste of time!'), use 'needn't have + V3'.

Tip Skor Exam: Skipped = didn't need to; Done needlessly = needn't have + V3!

Which offender noun describes someone who deliberately sets fire to a building or forest?

Huraian: An 'arsonist' intentionally starts fires to damage property; a 'burglar' breaks in to steal, a 'shoplifter' steals from store shelves, and a 'trespasser' enters land illegally.

Soalan 2 / 20 +10 XP

Which sentence uses 'rob' and 'steal' ACCURATELY?

Huraian: You 'rob' a place or person (the jewellery shop), and you 'steal' the items taken (twenty gold rings).

Soalan 3 / 20 +10 XP

A suspect forced open the museum skylight and accidentally triggered the security siren. Which Unit 6 phrase fits?

Huraian: 'Set off the alarm' means triggering a security siren or warning bell.

Soalan 4 / 20 +10 XP

What does the idiom 'do time' mean in a Unit 6 news article?

Huraian: 'Do time' is a common crime idiom meaning to spend time in prison.

Soalan 5 / 20 +10 XP

Select the accurate sentence for a SINGLE successful past police capture yesterday.

Huraian: Affirmative single successful events in the past take 'were able to' or 'managed to', never bare affirmative 'could'.

Soalan 6 / 20 +10 XP

Detective Lim printed fifty colour copies for a witness, only to learn the case had already been dropped an hour earlier. Which fits best?

Huraian: Because Lim actually completed the unnecessary printing, 'needn't have + V3' expresses wasted past effort.

Soalan 7 / 20 +10 XP

Complete the deduction: 'No glass was broken and the smart lock shows zero damage — the suspect ______ used a spare keycard.'

Huraian: Strong logical certainty supported by clean forensic evidence uses 'must have + V3'.

Soalan 8 / 20 +10 XP

Rule out a suspect using a solid alibi: 'Hafiz was speaking live on television in Penang at 9 p.m., so he ______ broken into the Johor office.'

Huraian: Logical impossibility based on a contradiction or alibi uses 'can't / couldn't have + V3' (never 'mustn't have').

Soalan 9 / 20 +10 XP

Which modal expresses weak negative possibility ('maybe the witness did NOT notice the number plate')?

Huraian: Weak negative possibility ('maybe not') requires 'may not / might not have + V3', because 'couldn't have' means '100% impossible'.

Soalan 10 / 20 +10 XP

Complete this hindsight criticism: 'You ______ left the ground-floor window unlatched before going on holiday.'

Huraian: 'Shouldn't have + V3' expresses criticism or regret about a careless past action.

Soalan 11 / 20 +10 XP

Which negative past ability sentence IS grammatically acceptable?

Huraian: While affirmative single past success bans 'could', NEGATIVE past inability accepts both 'couldn't' and 'wasn't able to'.

Soalan 12 / 20 +10 XP

Contrast obligation vs prohibition: 'Visitors ______ enter the forensic tape area; it is strictly sealed off.'

Huraian: Strict prohibition ('strictly sealed off') uses 'mustn't', whereas 'don't have to / needn't' mean optional.

Soalan 13 / 20 +10 XP

Identify the offender: Someone who enters private factory grounds without permission, without stealing or burning anything, is a ______.

Huraian: A 'trespasser' enters someone else's land or property unlawfully without permission.

Soalan 14 / 20 +10 XP

Complete BOTH blanks accurately: 'Police ______ locate the suspect via CCTV, and he ______ fled on foot because his motorbike stayed parked outside.'

Huraian: Specific past success requires 'were able to', and the motorbike still parked outside logically proves he 'must have' fled on foot.

Soalan 15 / 20 +10 XP

What distinguishes a 'burglar' from a 'vandal'?

Huraian: Burglary targets illegal entry to steal; vandalism is intentional destruction or defacement of property.

Soalan 16 / 20 +10 XP

Choose the strongest formal cause-and-effect sentence for an SPM crime-prevention essay.

Huraian: Objective phrasing ('Installing bright street lighting... deters opportunistic theft') maintains formal analytical register.

Soalan 17 / 20 +10 XP

Which transition fits formally between deterrence and youth prevention? 'Stricter patrols discourage street theft. ______, community mentorship addresses root causes.'

Huraian: 'Furthermore' cleanly introduces a complementary preventive argument in formal academic essays.

Soalan 18 / 20 +10 XP

Which thesis statement demonstrates Band 5 formal structure for a crime essay?

Huraian: A clear two-pronged thesis ('visible community patrols' + 'long-term youth guidance') previews balanced essay paragraphs formally.

Soalan 19 / 20 +10 XP

Complete cleanly: 'Because the store upgraded to keyless lockers, staff ______ carry heavy brass keys anymore.'

Huraian: Present absence of necessity uses 'don't have to' or 'needn't'.

Soalan 20 / 20 +10 XP

Spot the ONLY sentence that is 100% accurate across Unit 6 modals and crime lexis.

Huraian: Option D accurately uses 'shoplifter', 'set off', 'were able to' (single past success), and 'stolen jacket'.
